# Eval: what the classifier pipeline actually detects

reproducible: `.venv/bin/python tools/eval.py --dual`.

## What was tested

The same steps the live system runs, on saved files: `server/preprocess.py`
(600 Hz high-pass, per-window gain), the YAMNet TFLite model the iPhone app
ships, `server/labels.py` (label map, thresholds, k-of-n hits, cooldowns),
and `server/state.py`. A clip counts as detected when the HUD would have
shown the expected label. Alarm and siren count as the same tier, since both
produce the full-screen ALARM.

Clips, all 16 kHz mono in `server/clips/` (sources in `SOURCES.md`):

| class | clips | from |
|---|---|---|
| alarm | 3 clock alarms + 1 real smoke alarm recorded through the G2 (`plugin/recordings/alarm_1.wav`) | ESC-50, Wendy |
| siren | 3 | ESC-50 |
| doorbell | 6 (two-tone, cheap electronic, electronic, mechanical, old "tring", chime) | Wikimedia Commons |

Caveat that matters: 21 of the 22 clips are studio recordings, not sound that

## Results: clips detected, by preprocessing setting

| setting | alarm | siren | doorbell | knock | horn | dog | false cues on 6 noise clips |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| raw audio, no filter | 2/4 | 2/3 | 3/6 | 3/3 | 2/3 | 3/3 | 0 |
| high-pass 300 Hz + gain | 3/4 | 2/3 | | 2/3 | 2/3 | 3/3 | 0 |
| high-pass 600 Hz + gain (server today) | 4/4 | 3/3 | 2/6 | 0/3 | 1/3 | 3/3 | 0 |
| both: 600 Hz filtered AND raw, max score per label | 4/4 | 3/3 | 4/6 | 3/3 | 2/3 | 3/3 | 0 |

Median time from clip start to HUD, dual setting: alarm 1.0 s, siren 1.0 s,
knock 1.0 s, horn 1.0 s, dog 2.5 s, doorbell 3.2 s (doorbells ring twice;
the second ring is what crosses 2 of 3 windows).

The one real G2 recording, the smoke alarm: raw audio never detects it (the
model hears "Throbbing, Music"). With the 600 Hz high-pass it shows ALARM at
1.0 s and holds to the end. At 300 Hz it shows at 1.5 s with a best score of
only 0.15. So the high-pass is not optional for the G2.

## What this means

1. **The 600 Hz high-pass is required for alarms through the G2 and it
   deletes knocks.** Knocks are low-frequency transients; filtering at 600 Hz
   takes them from 3 of 3 to 0 of 3. Horns drop too. No single cutoff serves
   both: 300 Hz loses the real alarm, 600 Hz loses every knock.
2. **Running the model twice per window fixes it.** Once on the filtered
   window, once on the raw window, keep the higher score per label. On the
   same clips that goes from 13 of 22 to 19 of 22 with no new false cues,
   and the real G2 alarm is unchanged at 1.0 s. YAMNet takes well under
   100 ms per window on the iPhone, so two runs every 0.5 s is fine.
3. **Doorbells are the weak class.** 4 of 6 with the dual path. The two misses
   are a chime that YAMNet calls "Bell / Singing bowl" (doorbell score 0.26,
   threshold 0.5) and an old mechanical "tring" it calls "Telephone /
   Bicycle bell" and we show as ALARM. For the demo, use a two-tone
   electronic doorbell clip; those detected 3 of 3. Do not lower the
   doorbell threshold to catch the chime; "Bell" is too common in rooms.
4. **One siren-like horn shows as siren.** An air-horn style clip reads as
   "Ambulance (siren)" at 0.89. That is the model, not us. The demo horn
   should be a short car honk; both of those detected at 1.0 s.
5. **Zero false cues on 6 noise clips** under every setting, including a
   vacuum cleaner and a crying baby. The strict thresholds and 2-of-3 rule
   are doing their job.

## Recommended change for the classifier app (not adopted)

Decision, Sun 2026-10-18, 9 am: keep the single filtered path. The demo is
siren, dog bark, and speech, the three that detect every time. Changing the phone
app three hours before the deadline was judged not worth the risk. The
change below stays here as the measured next step.


In the classifier, for each 0.5 s window:

```
scores_filtered = yamnet(normalize(highpass(window)))
scores_raw      = yamnet(window)
scores          = max per class
```

Everything downstream stays as it is. In `fake_classifier.py --model` this is
a few lines in `Yamnet.top()`; in the iPhone app it is a second
`classify` call on the unfiltered buffer. `tools/eval.py --dual` is the
reference implementation.
